# The Annual Strategy Offsite

There is a rule in organisational life that has survived decades of evidence against it: *get the leadership team offsite and transformation will happen.*

The hotel is booked. The facilitator is hired. The Post-its are purchased in bulk. The energy in the room is genuine. People are away from their desks, their defences are lower, their ambition is higher. Ideas flow. Commitments are made. Photographs are taken of the whiteboard.

Then Monday arrives.

## How the Grip Tightens

The rule tightens through repetition. The first offsite feels productive. Genuine insights emerge. Real conversations happen. But the follow-through is partial. Some actions are taken. Most are absorbed by the daily system.

The next year, another offsite is scheduled. The tone is slightly different: "This time we will follow through." The same pattern repeats. Insights, energy, commitment, decay.

By the third year, the offsite has become a ritual. The organisation knows the pattern. Attend, engage, return, forget. The cynics arrive pre-armed. The optimists arrive pre-exhausted.

## The Release

The release requires accepting an uncomfortable truth: **transformation is not an event. It is a metabolism.**

Events can catalyse. A well-run offsite can surface truths that the daily rhythm suppresses. But the transformation does not happen in the room. It happens in the Tuesday afternoon decision. The Wednesday standup. The Thursday one-on-one where a manager chooses to do something differently.

If the daily system does not change, the offsite is theatre. Beautiful, expensive, well-catered theatre.

## The Residue

The residue is cynicism. Each offsite that produced announcements without changes taught the organisation something: transformation is something leaders talk about, not something the system does.

Reversing that lesson takes years of small, visible, daily changes that nobody photographs and nobody puts on a slide.

> *"One who acts without understanding the means will find themselves as lost as one who understands but never acts."* (Thirukkural 478)

The offsite provides understanding. Only the daily system provides means.
