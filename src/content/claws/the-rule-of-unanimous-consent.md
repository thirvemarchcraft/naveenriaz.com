# The Rule of Unanimous Consent

You learned it early. Maybe a mentor said it, maybe a leadership book prescribed it: *get everyone on board before you move.*

It sounds wise. Democratic. Respectful.

It is a trap.

The rule of unanimous consent turns every dissenter into a veto holder. Not because they intend to block, but because the system gives them that power by default. When "everyone must agree" is the operating standard, one person's hesitation becomes the entire organisation's stasis.

## How the Grip Tightens

The pattern is predictable. A decision is proposed. Most of the room nods. One person raises a concern. Instead of acknowledging the concern and proceeding, the group pauses. A follow-up meeting is scheduled. Then another. The concern morphs. New concerns emerge. The original decision loses momentum.

By the time everyone "agrees," the window has closed. The market moved. The competitor shipped. The best people, the ones who wanted to act, have either left or stopped caring.

## The Release

The release comes when you separate two things that were incorrectly fused: **consent** and **consensus**.

Consensus means "I agree with this decision."
Consent means "I can live with this decision, even if I would have chosen differently."

The first requires unanimity. The second requires sovereignty: each person's willingness to support a direction they did not personally champion, because they trust the process that produced it.

## The Residue

The grip does not release cleanly. Years after recognising the trap, the reflex still fires. Before making a decision, there is a pull to poll the room. To check. To make sure nobody objects. The intellectual understanding arrives instantly. The embodied release takes much longer.

> *"Pursuing virtue steadily along the right path is the mark of lasting greatness."* (Thirukkural 463)

The right path requires someone to choose it. Not everyone to agree on it.
